Dearest friends,

The camp is established. The tents rose swiftly and without calamity — a feat I attribute to superior planning and only partially to luck. We are, at last, entrenched.

This evening we feasted. My brother-in-arms and brother-in-law, kin of my dear Cindy, manned the cookfire and produced cheeseburgers and hot dogs of remarkable quality. Every soul ate their fill. There were no complaints, which among children is the highest honor a cook may receive.

I am pleased to report a great alliance has formed: our hound Indie and Tommie's hound Knox have become the dearest of comrades. They patrol the grounds together as one unit.

The adults have taken to the rations with the enthusiasm of soldiers granted furlough — jello shots, Long Drinks, Surfside Vodkas, and Lucky Ones flow freely through camp. Morale has never been higher, and the quartermaster reports supplies are holding, for now.

The recruits were marched to the local playground and returned exhausted, which was precisely the objective.

I have armored myself in bug spray of the strongest grade — forty percent DEET. The mosquitoes may take other men tonight, but they shall not take me.

We now retire to the fire, where marshmallows shall be roasted and consumed until sleep claims us all.

On the morrow, we launch a naval campaign. The company shall take to the river in a flotilla of rafts, kayaks, and canoes — hounds aboard — to drift with the current, strengthen the bonds of our fellowship, and partake once more of the aforementioned rations. I anticipate no casualties, save perhaps a sunburn or two.

Yours in fellowship and mild inebriation,
— Cpt. Tony

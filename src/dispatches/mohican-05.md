Dearest friends,

The evening has been one of feasting, wildlife, humiliation, and song. I shall recount it all.

Supper was commanded by the eldest of the young recruits — Tommie and Joey, now grown soldiers in their own right — who manned the pie irons and produced campfire pizzas made to order. They were delicious, and there was something stirring in watching the siblings labor side by side at the fire. The bonds of family, forged in melted cheese.

Nature graced us with a visitation: a red-headed woodpecker, perched upon a tall stump riddled with the holes of woodpeckers past — a monument to generations of industrious birds. Cindy was enchanted. It was, I confess, beautiful.

I must now report an incident concerning myself. Following supper, I fell into a slumber of tremendous depth in a chair by the fire. I am told — by Tommie and Carol, who showed no mercy — that my mouth hung open at such an alarming angle that I appeared to be suffering a medical event. I have no defense. A soldier sleeps where he can, and apparently how he must.

The hounds Indie and Knox continue their tireless watch, defending the camp from all canine trespassers with thunderous barking and leashes strained to their absolute limits, striking terror into man and beast alike. Their vigilance is total. Their volume, regrettably, is also total.

Young Macie paraded Indie about the grounds in the manner of a champion show dog, and I must say the hound carried herself with surprising elegance. A future career may await them both.

The night concluded gloriously: karaoke at the campsite. Mattie, fearless as ever, took the stage and delivered "Stupid Song" by Olivia Rodrigo, then followed with "Deja Vu" to rousing applause. The girl knows no stage fright. She will go far.

And now, we retire early. Sunshine, generous rations of drink, and six hours of river paddling have laid this company low. Even the strongest among us can barely keep our eyes open.

Until the morning, dear friends.

Yours in exhaustion and song,
— Cpt. Tony

Dearest friends,

This shall be my final letter from the field.

The night was merciful at last. Sleep came easily — whether owing to honest exhaustion or the lingering effects of the rations, I shall let history judge. Our hound Indie stirred only a few times, offering but a handful of soft barks into the darkness before returning to her post among the blankets. And I — I slept in. After two nights of struggle against the cursed air mattress, I count this among the campaign's greatest victories.

Now we take our breakfast, and then comes the final trial: the packing of the wagon. Do not be deceived by its mundane appearance. Packing is the crucible in which the bonds of family and friendship are truly tested. Many a company has survived the river only to fall apart over whose cooler goes where.

If this letter is my last, know that it was either the packing that ended us, or some misfortune of the road home — dysentery, a broken axle, or the fording of a river gone wrong, as befell so many on the trail to Oregon.

But should we make it home — and I believe we shall — let this ledger stand as the record of a family's wonderful and loving expedition to Mohican Reservation. There was feasting. There was song. There was a shoe, lost and miraculously found. There were hounds who guarded us fiercely and children who swam and laughed and sang without fear. We had a great time, every soul among us, and we shall absolutely return.

It has been the honor of my life to serve as your correspondent.

Homeward bound,
— Cpt. Tony

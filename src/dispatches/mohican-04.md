Dearest friends,

The river campaign is complete, and I am pleased to report it a resounding triumph.

At 0900 hours we boarded the transport van to the drop-off point, piloted by a driver whom I can only describe as the oldest man in the world. He delivered us safely, and I suspect he has been delivering rivergoers safely since before the invention of the river.

Once upon the water, our vessel quickly distinguished itself. I assumed command of the music, and by unanimous decree of the children, we were christened the "party raft." I wear this honor humbly.

Cindy and Tommie manned a canoe with the hounds aboard, and the spectacle was most entertaining. Indie, on her maiden voyage, proved a hesitant but ultimately willing sailor. She regards jumping into the water with deep suspicion, and I respect her caution.

We made port at several stations along the route for necessities — facilities, and the replenishment of beer and spirits. The second stop featured a dog park, where the hounds were granted liberty to run wild in safety.

I must report one casualty: Mattie took a tumble while marching through the river and injured her knee. She endures with courage and is otherwise well.

The sandwiches, prepared by the incomparable Cindy, were devoured entirely at the first stop. The men speak of them still.

All the young recruits were given their turn in the water alongside the rafts, and much joy was had.

Now, the tale that shall be told for generations: young Raymond, grandchild of Mike, lost his shoe to the river. A search was mounted. Nothing was found. Many tears were shed. Then young Micah declared, "It's probably under the raft" — a theory we all dismissed as the fancy of a child. Minutes later, the shoe emerged from beneath the raft, bobbing to the surface in vindication. Micah was right. We were fools. History will remember him kindly.

After six hours upon the water, we have returned to camp. All hands are dried, all gear is stowed, and tonight we feast upon campfire pie iron pizzas.

Yours in victory and slight sunburn,
— Cpt. Tony

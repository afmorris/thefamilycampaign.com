Dearest friends,

I write to you at dawn, weary but unbroken.

The night was cruel to us. Rest, that most precious of rations, was in short supply throughout the camp.

Private Joey suffered the gravest misfortune: his hammock mount gave way in the darkness and collapsed beneath him. He was forced to retreat to the wagon, where he passed the night in exile among the seat cushions. He endures.

Our hound Indie, on her maiden campaign, proved a vigilant but exhausting sentry. Every snapped twig, every distant owl, every rustle of the unfamiliar wilderness roused her — and she, in turn, roused us. She is skittish in this strange country, and I cannot fault her devotion to our safety, though I dearly wish she would ration it.

As for myself: the air mattress remains my oldest enemy. We have fought many battles, and I have never once emerged victorious. Last night was no exception. I rose at 6:30 and now sit in the wagon, restoring my telegraph device to full power.

But the morning brought salvation. Mike commanded the cookfire and produced a breakfast worthy of generals — pancakes both plain and blueberry, and bacon in generous supply. Spirits, once low, have rallied magnificently.

The camp is packed. The flotilla awaits. To the river!

Yours in exhaustion and anticipation,
— Cpt. Tony

Dearest friends,

Seven hours. Seven hours this household has dwelt in darkness, and I write to you now with news worthy of the wait: the breakthrough has come.

The lower box — the new garrison — stands complete and wired. It resisted, of course. The position would not hold fast until I drove it into the stud itself, and even then the first screws proved unworthy of the task, stripping out like green recruits at first fire. This necessitated a resupply run to the depot, where I secured screws of proper caliber and, while the wagon was hitched, a store of large Wago connectors — fine modern instruments that have made honest work of the chaos within the box. It was but a single trip, and I count that discipline among the day's quieter victories.

The upper box, as if in apology for its brother's insolence, came together with hardly a fight. All is connected. All is tested. All is good.

There remains only the final maneuver, known to every veteran of these walls: the shoving of the wires. It is an inelegant business — no manual describes it honestly — but the wires must yield, the plates must seat, and the screws must turn. I go now to finish it.

Yours at the edge of victory,
— Cpt. Tony
